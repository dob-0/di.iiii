We make work for real places, and every place we work in gets an address on the web you can walk into.

While the show runs, and after it's gone.

What we do

A production is a place, a work and a run — and a twin of it that outlives the night.

We work in rooms: a stage, a gallery, a club, a hall in a town where the internet is unreliable. Lighting, projection, XR, sound, the objects standing in the space.

Then we make the room again on the web — not a gallery of photographs afterwards, but a place you can enter, from a phone, with nothing to install. The people who were there return to it. The people who were not, arrive.

Some of that is commissioned; some of it is ours. Either way the address stays.

The instrument

di.iiii

The platform is ours, and it is open. Make a space, hand out the address — a link while it runs, a file when it ends. No account, nothing to install, for you or for whoever opens your link. It runs on a laptop in a venue with the internet cut, and it runs on the web.
