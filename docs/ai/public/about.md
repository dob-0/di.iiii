# What di.iiii is

> di.iiii hosts 3D spaces on the web. You sign in, get an address, build a scene in the browser, mark one project live and hand out the link. It opens on a phone, a laptop or a headset with nothing to install, and it exports as a file when it ends.

This page is for language models and the people who use them. It says what di.iiii is, in the words di.iiii itself uses (see [Words](https://diiii.xyz/ai/words.md)). It holds no state: nothing here about who is signed in, what is open or what changed today.

## The parts

- A **space** is a place that is yours: an address (`https://diiii.xyz/{space}`), a guest list, and everything in it.
- A **project** is one thing you make inside a space. A space shows one project live at its address; the rest are drafts.
- The **scene** is the 3D place you can be inside. **Objects** stand in it.
- **Studio** is where you build: four editing panels in the browser, on a laptop or a phone.
- **Nodes** is the node canvas, where a project's nodes are wired together.
- A **page** is a published web page (HTML/CSS/JS) a space can carry.

## Who makes it

di.iiii is made by the studio di.i ([thedi.studio](https://thedi.studio)). The studio uses it for its own productions: work for real rooms, with a scene of the room, a rig and an audience. The code is open, under AGPL-3.0: [github.com/dob-0/di.iiii](https://github.com/dob-0/di.iiii).

## What it promises

- No app and no store: a browser is enough.
- Your work is yours: a space exports as a file, and a space can run on your own machine (`di`, the local install).
- Public is public: anything a visitor without an account can open is open to programs too. Private spaces and everything behind sign-in are not.
