## 2026-10-28 — head light: a flashlight that follows the view in walk mode, off unless the project turns it on

- Built: `worldState.headLight = { enabled: true }` (project document, next to `spawn`, `fog`, `walkableAreas`; set with a `setWorldState` op) gives a walking visitor a three.js SpotLight at the camera's world pose: ~35 degree cone, 10 m, decay 2, warm white (`#ffe6bd`), 60 cd. L toggles it, a square Head light button toggles it on a phone, it is off at the start of every walk and the state is never saved. Code: `src/components/headLight.js` (field, key, constants), `HeadLight.jsx` (the light), `useHeadLight.js` (state and key), wired in `LiveProjectScene.jsx`. The schema change is in both copies (`src/shared/projectSchema.js`, `shared/projectSchema.cjs`) with a case in `serverXR/src/schemaSync.test.js`.
- Keys (owner, 2026-10-28, ledger N1445: "if every one has their own lets keep our how that is"): Fly keeps F in every project, exactly as on dev; the head light takes L, and only does something in a project that turns it on. Round 1 had moved Fly to G in head light projects and put the light on F; that is gone. No key other than L is new, and Q, E and the rest are not touched. The key words of the hint lines live in `src/components/walkKeys.js` (`walkKeyHintText`), so the code, the hints and the wiki have one source; `walkKeysSeams.test.js` holds the scene, the rig builder, the landing line and the wiki to it.
- Shadows follow quality. High (renderer shadows on): the light casts, the solid meshes join the pass through `ShadowCasting lamps={false}` (no room lamp becomes a caster) and `dressForShadows` counts the head light's map against the texture-unit cap. Low (`outputRenderSettings` drops shadows): no shadow map. The probe in the browser read `shadowMap.enabled` true / `castShadow` true in High and false / false in Low.
- Low quality does not stop at walls, by measurement (`scripts/head-light/lab.html`, run by `lab-run.cjs` on the RTX 3080 laptop GPU, three r186, thresholds written in the file before the first run). T1, what the walker sees, High against Low in the same view: 0 % of pixels differ by more than 2 % of the peak, mean difference 0 % (limits 1 % and 0.5 %): the shadow changes nothing the walker sees. T2, light behind a wall (target 1 m behind an invisible wall, over the same target with no wall): High 0.000 (limit 0.05, pass); Low 1.000, the wall does not stop it (no pass claimed). The light is at the eye, so every surface the walker sees is a surface the light sees; what the beam does behind a wall shows only from another viewpoint (none exists in walk mode). Limit, stated plainly: a second viewer of the same scene (a camera that is not the walker's) would see the leak in Low.
- Thrown away: the first version clamped `light.distance` to the first wall along the view (+0.35 m). T3: the lit wall kept 23 % of its luminance under the clamp (limit 90 %), because three.js windows a light to zero at its range; it also cast a ray at every mesh every few frames. It was replaced by the plain light.
- Headset: not built, not tested. The light reads the app camera's world pose, which WebXR writes at render time, so it should follow the head one frame late; there is no key or button in immersive mode, so nothing there can switch it on.
- Pictures (`scripts/head-light/seed.mjs` + `shots.cjs`, scratch stack, High and Low, desktop and phone, light colour scheme): the walk HUD has one fixed dark-glass style and no light theme, so the light-scheme picture is the same look.
- Lessons for the next scene author: an authored object gets a walk-mode idle bob and spin unless `components.animation = { mode: 'static' }`; a primitive's position is its base, not its centre; `di-dev up <tree> --api scratch` needs `npm --prefix serverXR ci` first; three's package `exports` hides `build/three.module.js` from `require.resolve`.
- Not done, owed: frame time of the shadow pass and of the first toggle (the lit materials recompile once; the shader warm-up holds the last frame meanwhile) on a real phone; a Studio control for the field (today only an op sets it); a headset toggle; an offset flashlight (held low, to the side) would make shadows visible, and is a design call, not built.
