# MOXIR: the hall measured from above (2026-10-07)

The owner said: *"the columns are still not right … check the building sizes from Google Maps and other sources"*.

This document measures the MOXIR hall (Charentsavan; owner's pin 40.406524, 44.636560) from above, using independent
sources. Columns cannot be seen from above. The roof shows the plan grid instead: the roof edges, the lanterns, the
expansion joint and the roof's repeating rhythm.

- **Measured record:** `scripts/place/rigs/moxir-aerial-2026-10-07.json`. It is a new layer and edits no older file.

## Verdict

**The plan grid holds.** The roof shows 4 spans of 24.0 m, a 6 m rhythm and 18 bays (108 m), with the joint near the
middle. The roof does **not** show a different pitch or a different number of spans. Two independent satellite captures
agree on the hall's size to within 0.1 m. If the columns are wrong, the error is in how the columns are placed on this
grid (the joint pair, the end-wall columns, an extra column), not in the grid. The photographs and the tape on 10-08
have to settle that.

## Method

The sources are each fetched by a subcommand of `aerial_measure.py`. Each output JSON records its licence and fetch time.

| Source | What | Licence / terms |
|---|---|---|

| Esri World Imagery Wayback 44873 | Maxar WorldView-3, 2020-10-30, native 0.31 m (Esri metadata `SRC_DATE 20201030, SRC_RES 0.31`) | Esri terms of use: display with attribution, no redistribution. Images stay private, tiles stay in the scratch cache. |
| Esri World Imagery Wayback 22252 = current World Imagery here | Maxar GeoEye-1, 2024-08-22, native 0.46 m | as above |
| Not used | **Google**: their terms forbid scraping, so the owner measures it himself (below). **Bing**: needs a key, and its terms for tile analysis were not checked. **Sentinel-2**: 10 m pixels cannot resolve a 6 m rhythm. Of the 4 Wayback releases that differ at the pin, only these 2 are distinct captures. | — |

**Steps:**

1. **Georeferencing.** Coordinates go WGS84 → UTM 38N (EPSG:32638) with PROJ (pyproj 3.8.0). Imagery is Web Mercator
   tiles (EPSG:3857 tile maths). Each output pixel's lon/lat is mapped to a tile pixel and resampled bilinearly
   (OpenCV remap) straight into the hall frame at 0.25 m per pixel. Esri serves this place only up to **z18 = 0.455
   m/px**: z19 returns 404 for Wayback and a placeholder for the current layer. So 0.455 m is the real ground sampling,
   and 0.25 m is only the drawing grid.
2. **Footprints.** Each footprint gets the minimum-area oriented bounding box (rotating calipers, Freeman & Shapira 1975;
   shapely 2.2.0) in UTM metres. Bearings come from the geodesic inverse (Karney 2013). Grid→true uses the meridian
   convergence, −0.23° here.
3. **Orientation.** Projection-profile deskew (the Radon method used for document skew) picks the bearing that makes
   the across and along edge profiles sharpest, in steps of 0.1° over ±2°.
4. **Edges.** Mean intensity profiles are smoothed with a Gaussian (σ 0.5 m). Edges are the extrema of the first
   derivative (scipy `find_peaks` on |d/dx|), with parabolic sub-pixel interpolation. The analysis is run on the whole
   roof and on strips: each span, each lantern, each lantern shadow band.
5. **Bay pitch.** Each along-hall strip profile is detrended (a 12 m Gaussian is subtracted) and Hann-windowed. Then:
   - the FFT power peak is found in the 3–30 m band, with ± from the half-width at half maximum, together with the
     autocorrelation;
   - the **phase test**: the 6 m and 12 m Fourier components are taken in 16 strips, each 3 m wide, over spans 1 and 4.
     A real bay rhythm has the same phase in every strip. The Rayleigh test (Zar 1999, eq. 27.4) checks this;
   - in 2020 the 6 m maxima lie 3.8 m from the joint line, mod 6. Read as the top-chord lines of a square-on-square
     offset space frame (half a module from the column lines) with a ±0.5 m joint-line error, this is SUSPECTED, not
     proven.
6. **Lantern ends and shadows.**
   - **The shadow.** Each lantern casts a shadow that shows on the roof as an L-shaped band: 1.9 m wide along its NE side
     and 4.5 m long at its NW end (2020). The band reaches 1.8–1.9 m beyond the lantern's NE edge, which shows it is a
     cast shadow and not a dark lantern end.
   - **The NW ends** were read where the shadow ends and the sunlit lantern top begins.
   - **The SE ends** were read at the end of the NE shadow band, plus the shadow geometry (v₂ = band end + 4.5·δ/1.9,
     δ ≈ 0.9 m inside the strip, which adds about 2.2 m).
   - **Lantern height** comes from the shadow vector: 4.88 m pointing to 347°. The sun's azimuth (167°) and elevation
     (34.8°) on 2020-10-30 come from the NOAA solar position equations, giving 3.40 ± 0.43 m. This is **UNVALIDATED**
     because the capture time is not published, so the azimuth is inferred from the shadow itself.

## Limits

- **The roof shows the roof module, not the columns.** A 6 m roof module over columns at 12 m looks the same from
  above. Only the photos and the tape can count columns.
- **The 6 m rhythm is in one capture only.** In 2020 it is weak in amplitude (median z ≈ 1 against nearby periods) but
  coherent across the roof. In 2024 the solar panels hide it.
- **Every length rests on a roof edge**, and the edge is ±0.5 m (one pixel at 0.455 m plus the parapet and eave). At the
  2020 SE end there are two edges 1.6 m apart (60.08 / 61.66). The outer one was taken because the 2024 capture (a
  single, strong edge) then gives the same length. With the inner one the length is 106.6, which is what the 09-28
  session used.
- **The positions of the column lines along the hall are inferred**, as the joint ± 6k. The joint itself is ±0.5–0.8 m
  (the roof line and the lantern-gap centre differ by 0.6 m).
- **The NW and SE end-wall asymmetry** (53.3 / 54.9 from the joint line) is in both captures but is only 2–3σ. The roof
  seam need not lie on the paired-column centre line.

## The owner's own check: Google Maps "Measure distance"

In Google Maps (satellite view), right-click → **Measure distance**, click the points and read the total. Click the
**roof edge**, not the dark shadow on the ground next to it. Zoom in until the roof edge is sharp. The imagery may be
newer and show the solar panels; the lanterns still show as long rectangles in the middle two spans.

1. **Length.** Along the long SW side (away from the road), from the roof's west corner to its south corner.
   Expect **108.2 ± 0.6 m**. The model is 109.6; OSM's polygon is 109.1.
2. **Width.** Along the NW end (the entry end), from the west corner to the north corner. Expect **97.3 ± 0.4 m**. The
   model is 97.4; OSM is 93.8.
3. **Span.** Measure across the hall from the NE long edge of one lantern line to the NE long edge of the other.
   Expect **24.0 ± 0.3 m**. Then measure the width of one lantern, edge to edge. Expect **12.3 ± 0.4 m**.
4. **Lantern and joint.** On one lantern line, measure along the hall from the NW roof edge to the NW end of the first
   lantern box. Expect **6.9 ± 0.4 m**; the model gives 9.05. Then measure the box's length. Expect **41.3 ± 1.0 m**;
   the model gives 38.5. If the straight line across the whole roof (the expansion joint) is visible, measure from the
   NW roof edge to it. Expect **53.3 ± 0.5 m**.

If his numbers differ from these by more than the ±, the satellite reading here is the thing to recheck first.

## Reproduce

```bash
P=~/tools/geo/.venv/bin/python; O=<scratch>; export AERIAL_CACHE=$O/cache
$P scripts/place/aerial_measure.py osm --out $O ; $P scripts/place/aerial_measure.py ms --out $O
$P scripts/place/aerial_measure.py releases --out $O          # distinct Wayback captures + Esri metadata
C="469134.28 4472953.73"                                       # OSM way 289841504 OBB centre, UTM 38N
for r in 44873 22252; do
  $P scripts/place/aerial_measure.py imagery --out $O --release $r --zoom 18 --half 90
  $P scripts/place/aerial_measure.py profile --out $O --release $r --zoom 18 --half 65 --center $C --bearing 144.83 \
     --strips="-48:-30;-19:-10;-6:1;5:14;25:43" --vstrips="-40:-8;14:50" --vrange -44 58
done
$P scripts/place/aerial_measure.py comb --out $O --release 44873 --zoom 18 --half 65 --strips="-50:-26;22:46" --ref 6.7 --vrange -45 59
$P scripts/place/aerial_measure.py shadow --out $O --measured scripts/place/rigs/moxir-aerial-2026-10-07.json
$P scripts/place/aerial_measure.py draw --out $O --release 44873 --zoom 18 --half 65 --measured scripts/place/rigs/moxir-aerial-2026-10-07.json \
   --site scripts/place/rigs/moxir-site-2026-09-28.json --layers osm,ms,model,measured --png ~/Downloads/moxir/aerial/5-combined-2020.png
```

The numbers in the measured record were read from the `profile`/`comb` outputs (in `docs/moxir/aerial-2026-10-07/`) by
the analyst. The rule that picks which edge is which is written in the method above. It is not automatic, and that is
owed if this is rerun on new imagery.
